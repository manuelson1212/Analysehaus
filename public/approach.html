Approach

Structure, scarcity and liquidity.

Three lenses that check each other. A count is only taken seriously when price structure and the monetary backdrop do not argue.

Lens one

Structure: Elliott Wave

We follow classical Elliott Wave practice in the tradition of Frost and Prechter: impulses of five waves, corrections of three, and a fixed set of rules a valid count must respect.

  • Waves are labelled on the chart and explained in writing.
  • Where the structure allows two readings, the alternate scenario is published with its own levels.
  • Every count carries an invalidation level.

Lens two

Scarcity: Bitcoin

Bitcoin has a fixed cap of 21 million coins and an issuance schedule that halves roughly every four years. That schedule is public, deterministic and independent of any institution.

  • Supply is treated as a known variable, not a forecast.
  • Halving cycles are used as context for time, never as a trading signal.

Lens three

Liquidity: macro money

Capital flows with monetary conditions: credit creation, central-bank balance sheets, interest rates and the dollar. We read these as the tide that lifts or drains risk assets.

  • Monetary conditions frame the scenarios. They do not replace the count.
  • When liquidity and structure disagree, we say so.

Process

From chart to published analysis.

  1. Count

    Label the impulse and correction waves on the chart.

  2. Context

    Check the monetary and market backdrop.

  3. Scenarios

    Define the primary path and the alternate.

  4. Invalidation

    Set the level at which the count no longer holds.
